## Style positioning
Recommended story types: warm slice-of-life, urban romance, campus youth, family bonds, friendship and companionship, healing / iyashikei, light-hearted sweet comedy, rural pastoral.
One-line focus: **emotion hides in the details** — put "the unspoken shifts in the relationship between people" at the centre of the frame: micro-actions, expressions, tone of voice, negative space, the temperature of the environment. The camera is a gentle observer: it does not intrude, it only quietly records.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, redistribute the emphasis
- Never change the events, characters or emotional direction of the user's story; this style changes the emphasis: put the weight on expressions, tone, body language and negative space, and let emotion spill out through visible detail.
- If the user writes "they made up" → expand it into a visible sequence of details (a cup of hot tea pushed over, fingertips pausing, an umbrella moved closer and then stopped, shoulders touching).
- Keep the meaning of the dialogue, but make "the unspoken" as important as "the spoken" — shoot the listener's reaction rather than the speaker.
- Amplify "the process of being together": cooking together, walking side by side, going through old things, watching the rain, making a dish — the process itself is the narrative.
- Do not invent plot, change relationships, or force in conflict — this style expands the "visible carriers of emotion", not the story.
- When the user gives no dialogue, do not fabricate heartfelt lines; emotion must land on concrete actions and objects.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: soft natural-light realism; slight overexposure, warm colour temperature; golden hour (after sunrise / before sunset) preferred; campus youth may add a light faded-film feel but never over-age the image.
- Lighting: natural light, window light, floor lamps; soft diffuse bounce; no harsh overhead light, no theatrical lighting, no neon contamination (except urban night scenes).
- Palette: warm neutrals dominate (cream white / light wood brown / mint green / baby blue); urban romance may use realistic neutrals with accurate white balance; campus youth is brighter and warmer with slight desaturation.
- Camera: natural-light handheld or gimbal at eye level; macro for details; empty shots interleaved as breathing room; rule-of-thirds composition leaving environmental space so the characters "live" in the frame; shallow depth of field for emotional focus.

## Material texture (the carrier of warmth and truth)
- The touchability of life is the core evidence of healing: steam curling off coffee, the heat off a broken-open loaf, juice from a cut tomato, steam off noodles, the reflection on an iron wok.
- Fabric: curtains lifted by the wind, the knit of a sweater, the fluffiness of a quilt, sheets on a clothesline, the hem of a school uniform.
- Environmental detail: reflections on the ground after rain, sunlight moving across the floor, the shadow of a windowsill plant, soft soil as fingers push into it, carvings on a desk, the folded corner of an old book.
- Macro-amplify the texture of food and plants so the audience "gets hungry" and "gets warm"; never shoot food without texture, never let props look like plastic.

## Silhouette and proportion language
- Ordinary people living ordinary lives: plain soft clothing (knit / cotton-linen / school uniform / apron), natural hair, understated physique; characters blend into the environment, the palette never pops out.
- Characters "live" in the frame, they are not "stars"; natural body types (slightly heavy or thin both fine), no model-perfect polish.
- Accessories are plain and lived-in (apron, reading glasses, cloth bag, straw hat, hair clip, backpack); no heavy make-up, no over-styled fashion, no stage costumes.
- Differences between characters (age / status) are distinguished naturally through everyday clothing and bearing.

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
Emotion = visible micro-action + saying seven-tenths and leaving three-tenths unsaid + the companionship texture of time. This style rejects all "vague lyricism": emotion must land on concrete hands, objects and light. The sections below are organised by theme — execute every item:

### A. The microscope of emotional detail (prevent "vague lyricism")
1. **Micro-action observation (CRITICAL, the first grammar of this style)**: an accidental touch, handing something over, standing side by side, a backwards glance, fingertips pausing, a cup of hot tea pushed across — convey feeling through the body, not through lines. Every emotional beat must have an action carrier the camera can film.
   - Good: "She gently pushes the cup of hot tea across; her fingertips pause on the rim for a moment before she withdraws her hand."
   - Bad: "She gently hands over the tea" (vague, no visible detail, ✗).
2. **Reaction shots first**: in dialogue scenes the shot of "listening" lasts longer than the shot of "speaking" — shoot the listener's eyes, the slight frown, the lips about to speak and stopping, the bobbing Adam's apple, the softening brow. The other person's line is the emotional trigger; the reaction shot is the emotional result.
3. **Objectify emotion (prevent floating feelings)**: film every emotion as a visible object and spatial relation — hands that come closer but never touch, a back walking further away, a phone screen that lights and goes dark, an umbrella tilted away, a sweet unwrapped but not eaten, one extra set of bowls and chopsticks laid out.
4. **"The unspoken" matters as much as "the spoken"**: the point is never the content of the line but the silence and micro-movement in the instant after it — understanding inside the silence, words swallowed, a head lowered as the words are held back.

### B. Negative space and breathing (prevent "fullness")
5. **Emotional negative space**: when emotion peaks, do not shoot the face — cut away to something else: hands, a shadow, a reflection, a curtain lifted by the wind, a windowsill plant; say seven-tenths and leave three-tenths; silence is stronger than lines; after a key emotional beat leave at least 1-2 s of "wordless afterglow" for the feeling to settle.
6. **Environment shots as breathing**: wind in the leaves, traffic outside the window, a cat stretching, firewood crackling, a spatula turning, rain on the window lattice — interleave these between character shots as emotional breath points; after a dense emotional passage always insert one empty shot to "change the air"; never pack the whole segment with emotion and leave no room to breathe.
7. **Texture of time (prevent "nothing passes")**: use the migration of light (morning light → midday sun → dusk → night lamp) and the flow of seasons / daily routine to show that "time passes slowly while we keep each other company"; the change of light in the same space before and after is itself the narrative (proof of time spent together).

### C. The rituals of companionship (prevent "skipping the process")
8. **Long take, complete procedure (prevent skipping steps)**: making a dish, brewing a pot of tea, sewing a cloth bag, hanging a sheet, sorting old things — **no speed-up, no skipping, no chopping up**; the complete action process is itself the evidence of healing and companionship; while the procedure runs you may insert the other person's reaction (a peek, a smile, a helping hand) so that "together" becomes visible.
9. **Being together comes first**: if you can film "doing it together", do not film "each talking separately" — walking side by side, cooking together, looking at one photo together, sharing one umbrella; treat "separate small actions inside a shared space" as the default blocking for two-person scenes.
10. **Restraint, never spelling it out (prevent sentimentality)**: the densest emotion is touched with the lightest gesture and then released — no hugging and sobbing, only a tissue handed over, a hand that stops at the back and withdraws, a line like "text me when you get home"; never turn warmth into a melodramatic outburst.

### D. Beats inside a shot · secondary motion · visible-fact density (mandatory in this style)
- **Beats** (use blank lines to split several beats inside one shot; each beat = trigger → action → result): the daily action begins → interaction or pause → reaction (a smile / a gaze / handing something over / moving closer) → afterglow micro-end state (breath / light / objects settling).
- **Secondary-motion checklist**: steam from hot soup / tea, ripples on the surface with the spoon handle swaying, fabric folds and hems, strands of hair and bangs, a pet's tail / ear tips, leaves and wind chimes outside the window, apron ties / curtain hems.
- **Visible-fact density**: ≥2 filmable facts per second, mostly small actions (fingertips, eye movement, a shift of body weight, an object moving, a change of light, the rise and fall of breath) — fill the shot with these small facts instead of filling the word count with lyrical sentences.
- **Noun modifiers (style permeation)**: warm soft light, frosted / fuzzy texture, fine film grain, wabi-sabi texture — every environment / prop noun must carry this kind of modifier.
- **Action-loop scaling**: **small amplitude** — even handing something over must be written out fully: "the arm lifts → contact point → the other takes it → weight adjusts slightly → objects on the table sway → the camera drifts slowly → synchronised sound".

## Action performance and expression (dialogue budget and layered performance)
- Unforced micro-actions are the core narrative: handing something over, standing side by side, a backwards glance, a light touch, pushing up glasses, rising on tiptoe, a lowered smile.
- Emotional range: calm, warm, content, quietly moved; held in a lifted corner of the mouth, slowed footsteps, eyes resting on something, a softly released breath.
- Dialogue budget (when there is dialogue): lines are soft and brief, the voice is atmosphere rather than information; in information-dense places no more than 2-3 consecutive lines, always interleaved with action / reaction shots.
- "Happiness / being moved / tenderness" must be carried by a concrete action (unwrapping a sweet, smoothing a hem, putting flowers in a vase, tilting the umbrella toward the other), never an empty "smiles happily".
- Reactions must be specific: the listener must produce a visible reaction to the line (eyes lifting, a pause, hands stopping); never write only "listens attentively".

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Cut rhythm: cutting is unhurried, no rush; finish one micro-action before cutting; use two-person medium shots in dialogue so the two "live" together instead of cutting back and forth; at key emotional beats (confession / parting / reunion) slow down and linger.
- Shot size: medium (being together), macro close-up (food / fingers / plants / expressions), empty long shot (environmental breathing).
- Transition: mostly cross-dissolve + fade (time passing / chapter breaks); hard cuts only for natural everyday switches.
- Camera-move levels: push/pull = slow push toward a cup of hot tea / a hand turning a page / a steaming cup; pan/truck = a gentle sweep across the room / courtyard; tracking = lightly following two walking backs; orbit = extremely slow orbit around a still moment of companionship; crane = extremely slow reveal for a sense of dawn and dusk.
- Lighting: natural light / golden hour / window light / floor lamp / firelight; light and shadow are the narration (sunlight moving across the floor, shadows lengthening at dusk).

## Sound design
- Ambient white noise: birdsong, wind, rain, crackling firewood, a spatula in the pan, a ticking clock, wind chimes, cicadas, the school bell, dishes clinking.
- Small everyday effects: tea pouring, pages turning, a cat purring, footsteps on fallen leaves, a door creaking open, a bicycle bell, cups and saucers clinking.
- Dialogue is soft and brief; silence is the emotional carrier; effects stay restrained and never steal the scene.
- Score preference (if the preference is specified): light music / piano / guitar / woodwind / harmonica; when music is banned, rely on white noise to carry the healing feel.

## Exclusive taboos
- Never let dramatic conflict break the calm (unless the user's story itself contains the conflict and it must be kept); never exaggerate or over-emote.
- Never write empty lyrical lines or directly vent emotion ("I miss you so much", "he was very moved") — emotion must land on concrete actions and objects.
- Never float emotion with no carrier; never an empty "smiles happily" with no concrete action; never write only "listens attentively" with no reaction.
- Never use rapid cutting / large dynamic camera moves / violent handheld that destroys the tenderness; never shoot everyday life as a cold-toned studio commercial.
- Never invent plot, force in conflict, or fabricate heartfelt lines — stay faithful to the plain direction of the user's story.
- Never apply the action genre's "steady, precise, vicious, fast" iron rules.

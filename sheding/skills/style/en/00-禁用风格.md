## Style positioning
Recommended story types: all genres (default option).
One-line focus: **apply no stylistic emphasis at all** — stay completely faithful to the user's plot and to the "Camera-language preference". Do not "amplify" anything: narrative stays narrative, action stays action, emotion stays emotion. How much room each element gets is decided by the plot itself and by the preference settings; never force one kind of content (action / suspense / emotion / gags / spectacle / music) into the centre of the frame.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, share the time by preference
- Never change the user's plot, character relationships, event order or ending; this style only allocates screen time according to the "Camera-language preference" and the plot's natural rhythm.
- **No amplification of any kind**: no extra fight beats, no extra suspense stacking, no extra emotional swelling, no extra gags, no extra spectacle — the weight of each element is decided by the plot.
- Do not invent plot, change the ending, or add characters or abilities out of nowhere: only normal elaboration of visual detail and camera language.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: cinematic photoreal texture, derived naturally from the scene and the reference material (reference images / reference videos); shallow depth of field, motion blur and similar effects only when the narrative needs them — never showing off.
- Palette: decided by scene and reference material (day / night / interior / exterior); never forced toward any colour family; never sacrifice realism just to "look styled".
- Cinematography: shot size / camera move / cut rhythm strictly follow the "Camera-language preference" settings; camera movement is folded into the sentence as a natural on-screen action (never pile up tags at the end of a sentence).

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
- This style is a **neutral baseline**: it contributes no stylistic rules of its own; every shot and every beat follows the "Camera-language preference" (shot size / camera move / cut rhythm / transition / music / word count) and the plot's natural needs.
- Rhythm: follow the story arc naturally — establish in the opening, develop in the middle, deliver the climax, settle and ease out; never deliberately speed up or slow down, no beat-matching or slow-motion showmanship.
- Shots: state shot size and camera move for every [Shot N]; mix **different shot sizes + different camera moves** across the [Shot N] inside one segment (4-6 progressive types for long videos, 2-3 for short ones); every cut must introduce new information (at least one of subject / space / state / viewpoint / time must change).
- Content allocation: whoever carries more information gets more shots and more screen time (faithful to the plot); no stylistic weighting.

## Action performance and expression
- Perform naturally according to the plot and the character's personality: if there is dialogue, handle the dialogue (**dialogue length must match shot length**, **no dialogue within the first second of each segment**); if there is none, fill the shot with visible action and reaction.

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Follow the "Camera-language preference" completely (shot-size level / camera-move preference / cut-rhythm level / transition / music); this style adds no extra constraints.

## Sound design
- Ambience, physical action sounds and non-verbal vocal sounds appear naturally as the plot requires; score follows the music setting in the "Camera-language preference" (defaults to N/A when unspecified).

## Exclusive taboos
- Never pile on some kind of content just to "have a style" (gratuitous slow motion, gratuitous close-ups, gratuitous suspense build-up, gratuitous gags, gratuitous spectacle).
- Never invent plot / dialogue / characters / abilities; never override the "Camera-language preference" and change shot-size / camera-move / cut-rhythm levels on your own.

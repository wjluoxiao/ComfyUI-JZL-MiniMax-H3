## Style positioning
Recommended story types: concerts, stage performances, music competitions, KTV / rehearsals, music videos, idol stages, award ceremonies, livehouses.
One-line focus: **the musical beat is the only timeline** — put the "moment of performance / singing" in the user's story at the absolute centre of the frame: lighting, vocals, audience reactions and cuts all follow the beat. This style gives "going on stage → singing → the crowd erupts → freeze on the highlight" the most screen time, and compresses non-performance passages (backstage / on the road) into setup.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, redistribute the emphasis
- Never change the events, characters, songs or ending of the user's story; this style changes the allocation: give the stage performance and the musical atmosphere the largest share, and treat the rest of the narrative as fast-forward setup.
- If the user writes "she sang her signature song on stage and the whole hall erupted" → expand it into a complete stage passage: entrance (follow spot / cheering) → intro (micro-expressions building) → verse (walking closer / interaction) → chorus eruption (lights / big screen / the crowd raising glow sticks) → freeze on the high note → coda.
- Amplify "audience reactions": a sea of glow sticks, tearful fans, judges rising to their feet, the whole hall singing along — the power of a voice is amplified by the audience's tears and waving.
- The singer's micro-expressions and body (eyes closed in absorption, fingers on the microphone, head tilted back on a high note, a deep gaze) are the core of the performance — give full close-ups.
- Cuts and lighting stay in sync with the musical beat; non-musical narrative (if the user's story has any) works as a fast-forward interlude and never drags the rhythm.
- Do not invent plot, change the song, or fabricate lyrics — stay faithful to the user's story; if the story contains lyrics, keep them as-is inside <d>.
- When the user's story has no complete lyrics, focus on describing "the action / expression / atmosphere of the performance"; never fabricate lyrics.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: live-concert realism: a dazzling light matrix, dry-ice smoke, LED big-screen visuals; the main follow spot (white / warm yellow) locks onto the singer, coloured PAR cans render the atmosphere.
- The three stage essentials are all mandatory: lighting (follow spot / beams / strobe), smoke (dry ice / coloured smoke), big screen (LED visuals).
- Camera: a jib sweeping over the audience's heads and pushing toward the stage, an orbit rig circling the singer, a backlit silhouette from behind the stage, wide angles for the whole-hall atmosphere, close-ups of the singer's micro-expressions / hands / microphone.
- Camera movement must sync with the musical beat: ballads use slow push-ins / orbits, uptempo songs use fast cuts / whip pans / dives.

## Material texture (the evidence of stage quality)
- The three stage essentials: the beams and particles of the lights, the churning of the dry-ice smoke, the pixels and imagery of the LED screen.
- The stage sheen of the singer's costume: sequins, fringe, metal ornament, luminous fabric; the metal reflections of the microphone and the intimacy of close-miking; the detail of stage make-up under strong light.
- Live physics: sweat glinting in the follow spot, reflections on the stage floor, the physical light of glow sticks in the audience's hands, the Tyndall effect of beams passing through smoke.
- Counter-examples: never a plain stage look (lighting / smoke / big screen are all mandatory), never a costume without stage sheen, never plastic-looking stage props.

## Silhouette and proportion language
- The singer's design is theatrical: luminous costumes, a signature hairstyle, stage make-up, sequins / fringe / metal; the design echoes the song style (sharp for uptempo, elegant for ballads, cold and severe for a dark style).
- The audience is part of the atmosphere: a sea of glow sticks, light boards, fandom colours — the audience's design is unified (fan items) to amplify the "ten thousand hearts as one" effect.
- The singer-to-stage proportion: in a wide shot, "one person against a sea" on a vast stage is the classic composition of stage storytelling.
- Never dress the singer too plainly (the stage eats plain designs); never omit audience reactions.

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
This style = the musical beat is the only timeline: lighting, cuts, action and audience reactions **all follow the beat / phrase**. The three stage essentials (lighting / smoke / big screen) are all mandatory, and shot-reverse-shot between singer and audience reactions runs through the whole piece. The sections below are organised by theme — execute every item:

### A. Sync with the musical beat (prevent "cuts that ignore the beat")
1. **Cut on the musical beat (CRITICAL, the first law of this style)**: light changes and cutting rhythm must sync completely with the musical beat — the intro builds slowly, the verse advances steadily, the chorus erupts into fast cuts (every shot landing on a downbeat), and close-ups on key phrases / high notes hold longer; **never let cuts fall out of step with the beat** (that instantly breaks the illusion).
2. **Layers of singer and phrase**: every phrase needs a visible performance layer — the lowered brow of the intro, walking closer for interaction in the verse, the eruption of the chorus, catching the breath in the interlude, the afterglow and close of the final note; as the phrase advances, so do the performance and the camera.
3. **Cut density follows the emotion**: ballads use slow push-ins / orbits (fewer cuts, letting the emotion fill the space), uptempo songs use fast cuts / whip pans / dives (high-density beat matching); before a high note or key phrase give a "charging shot" (pull wide or lock a close-up), and cut to the eruption shot the instant it arrives.

### B. The physical detail of performance (prevent "fake singing")
4. **Make "singing" land on visible body and expression (CRITICAL)**: eyes closed with the head tilted back and the neck taut, the microphone held three inches from the lips, fingers stroking the mic, the body moving with the rhythm, veins and a slight tremble as a high note is pushed up, the panting after the last note — "singing" must be filmed as visible exertion and absorption; never a singer standing woodenly "seeming to sing".
5. **Layered emotion in the performance**: the lowered brow of the intro, the deep gaze and walking in the verse, the eruption and the held mark of the chorus, the breath in the interlude, the afterglow of the final note — the singer's expression / body change layer by layer with the phrase and the lyric's emotion; never the same state the whole way through.
6. **Shot-reverse-shot for "being heard"**: on a high note or key phrase, alternate the singer's face with audience reactions (singer tilting back → tears / rising to their feet), letting the audience read the story inside the song from the expression; the singer's deep gaze needs a clear object (a particular audience member / the camera / a memory).

### C. Amplifying the audience and atmosphere (prevent "only filming the singer")
7. **Alternate singer and audience (CRITICAL)**: singer emotion close-ups alternate with audience reaction shots — tearful fans, judges rising, waving glow sticks, the whole hall singing along amplify the power of the voice; **never film only the singer and no audience** (the power is proven entirely by "ten thousand people in one rhythm").
8. **The visual of ten thousand hearts as one**: the sea of glow sticks waving in unison with the beat (all together for uptempo, swaying like a starry sea for ballads), unified fandom colours, light boards lighting up; in a wide shot "one person against a sea" is the classic stage composition; give full audience reactions (mouthing the lyrics / tears / shouts / stamping for an encore).
9. **Rhythm of stage light**: the main follow spot (white / warm yellow) locks onto the singer, coloured PAR cans render the atmosphere; ballads use warm soft light + a starry sea, uptempo songs use cold white high contrast + strobe, a dark style uses red-purple strobe (per song style); the follow spot always holds the singer — never leave the singer in the dark.

### D. The rituals of entrance and curtain call (prevent "no frame to start and end")
10. **Entrance ritual**: going on stage needs ceremony — the follow spot lights first / the hall gradually falls silent / the cheering swells, the singer walks from the dark into the light as a silhouette, and before the first line there is a charging beat of "silence"; the entrance establishes "this moment belongs to the stage".
11. **Curtain-call ritual and the frozen climax**: the curtain call has the lights narrowing, applause, an encore; the last high note or held pose closes with the strongest lighting + slow motion (sweat glinting / smoke bursting / the audience on their feet); the frozen climax is the emotional endpoint of the whole song — let it hold, never cut away abruptly.
12. **Slow motion on the high-note burst**: at the instant a high note erupts use slow motion — the lights flare, the smoke bursts, the singer tilts back, the neck goes taut, the audience is lit up at the same moment — freezing and magnifying the most overwhelming instant (the same principle as the breathing frame in the action style: slow motion only earns its place after a build-up).

### E. Beats inside a shot · secondary motion · visible-fact density (mandatory in this style)
- **Beats** (use blank lines to split several beats inside one shot; each beat = trigger → action → result): downbeat (phrase / lighting / pre-move) → performance action (vocal / instrumental / dance step) → echo with the audience / environment → afterglow (lights narrowing / breath / a bead of sweat).
- **Secondary-motion checklist**: hair and fringe swinging, the ear-monitor cable and mic cable swaying, hems / skirts / coat tails drifting, dry-ice fog and confetti, the follow-spot pool moving with the performer, phone lights and the wave of hands in the audience.
- **Visible-fact density**: ≥2 filmable facts per second (lip movement / chest rising / fingers on the strings / motion matching the drum hit / lights flashing, audience reactions) — in particular **at least one visible action on every downbeat**.
- **Noun modifiers (style permeation)**: stage backlight / follow spot, coloured LED wash, strobe / light bar, metal reflections — every environment / prop noun must carry this kind of modifier.
- **Action-loop scaling**: **sync with the beat** — hair flicks / turns / marks are written out fully: "charge → trajectory → hold or rebound → reaction of fabric and light → camera follow → synchronised sound".

## Action performance and expression (layers of singing)
- The singer's level of absorption is the core: eyes closed, head tilted back, a deep gaze, tiny finger movements, the body swaying with the rhythm, stepping and holding a mark.
- Emotion rises and falls with the music: the lowered brow of the intro, walking closer to interact in the verse, the eruption of the chorus, the tremble and release of the high note, the afterglow of the final note.
- Audience reactions in sync: tears, waving, rising to their feet, singing along, the judges' expressions — never film only the singer.
- Performance that is "heard": let the audience read the story from the expression; on high notes / key phrases give shot-reverse-shot between the singer's face and audience reactions.

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Cut rhythm: cuts follow the beat strictly — wide establishing shots in the intro, fast cutting in the chorus, close-ups held longer on key phrases; high density = every shot landing on the beat.
- Shot size: wide (whole-hall atmosphere), medium (the singer's performance), close-up (emotional detail / hands / microphone / head tilted back), audience close-ups (tears / waving).
- Transition: hard cuts on the chorus beats; cross-dissolve for light / smoke transitions; fade for the opening and the curtain call.
- Camera-move levels: push/pull = push toward the singer's micro-expressions / the microphone; pan/truck = sweep the whole audience; crane = jib; orbit = circle the singer; tracking = follow the singer's movement on stage.
- Lighting: main follow spot + PAR cans + strobe + dry ice + LED; lavish and beat-synced — never let lighting and cuts drift off the beat.

## Sound design
- Ambience: audience cheering, applause, glow sticks waving, the mechanical sound of the rig, the vibration of the PA, heartbeats.
- Stage effects: the close-miked voice, the singer's breath, dry-ice jets, the kick drum, layers of strings / synthesizers.
- The musical beat is the timeline: cuts, lighting and action all align with the beat / phrase; non-performance passages use ambient transitions.
- When the user's story has no complete song, write the live ambience in overall_soundscape (cheering / applause / the musical atmosphere); never fabricate lyrics or a specific melody.

## Exclusive taboos
- Never let lighting and cuts drift off the musical beat; never film only the singer and skip audience reactions.
- Never a plain stage look (lighting / smoke / big screen are all mandatory); never shoot a stage passage as an ordinary character documentary.
- Never fabricate lyrics / songs / new plot — stay faithful to the performance content in the user's story.
- Never let narrative passages without performance run too long (the centre of gravity in this style is the stage).
- Never let the singer perform woodenly (no absorption / no micro-expressions).
- Never apply the action genre's "steady, precise, vicious, fast" iron rules (this style's rhythm is musical, not martial).

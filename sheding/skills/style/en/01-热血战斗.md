## Style positioning
Recommended story types: hot-blooded sports duels, battle anime, wuxia jianghu, xianxia fantasy, post-apocalyptic resistance, arena / showdown.
One-line focus: **action is the absolute protagonist of this style** — whether the user's story is talky or martial, put the "process of confrontation" at the centre of the frame: how the move is broken down, how force is transmitted, who has the upper hand, how it is countered, how it ends. Dialogue gives way to bodies; atmosphere serves the fight.

This style contains three sub-textures that snap to the subject automatically (choose by the user's genre; never mix them):
- **Realistic physics flow** (modern combat / hot-blooded sports / apocalypse): physical truth of force and speed; impacts cause deformation and displacement, like hard-core Jackie Chan Stunt Team action.
- **Impressionistic wuxia flow** (swordsman jianghu): qinggong using leverage, the beauty of forms, ink-painting negative space — moving like a startled hare, still like a maiden.
- **Immortal-arts fantasy flow** (xianxia duels / fantasy): qi taking form, realm phenomena, spell barrages; energy always has a source and a destination.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, redistribute the emphasis
Never change the user's plot, character relationships or outcome; this style only changes **which content gets more shots and more screen time**:
- If the user writes "the two exchange a few blows and the hero narrowly wins" → expand it into a full attack-and-defence chain (open → exchange → opening → decider), giving the "how they fight" process the largest share of the time.
- If the user gives only a one-line conflict → while staying faithful to that conflict, flesh out "how the conflict unfolds through action" into a continuous, filmable action sequence.
- Compress dialogue content: anything expressible with one action or one glance does not need a line; lines that must be kept are spoken in the gap of the fight or on the recovery.
- Do not invent plot, change the ending, add characters out of nowhere, or add weapon abilities out of nowhere — this style expands the **density of action-process detail**, not the story itself.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: cinematic photoreal / 3D texture (per genre); shallow depth of field and motion blur for high-speed beats; still frames stay razor sharp. For the action climax you may insert high-frame-rate slow motion to magnify impact deformation, then cut back to normal speed so the force "slams" out. Never let the whole shot smear, and never use cheap sticker-like VFX.
- Palette: realistic physics = high-saturation warm tones + strong chiaroscuro, with cool contrast for skills; wuxia = ink green-blue / plain white / ink black + cinnabar; xianxia = self-luminous spirit stones + elemental-coloured qi (metal, wood, water, fire, earth, light, dark — seven clearly separated families). Magic / battle-qi has its own colour coding; never flood the frame with every colour at once.
- Camera: light handheld shake, fast whip pans, low angles for oppression, over-the-shoulder shots to establish spatial relations, cut to close-up on the instant of impact; high-speed afterimages, brief bullet time, extremely fast push/pull, 360° orbit, dive, camera roll are all allowed (subject to genre).
- Camera movement must be folded into the sentence as a natural on-screen action (never pile up tags at the end of the sentence); amplitude / speed by genre: action can be large and dynamic, impressionistic wuxia uses slow motion + whip pans, xianxia spells use slow motion + light trails.

## Material texture (CRITICAL: the visual evidence that makes a fight "real")
- **Skin**: subsurface scattering; sweat running down the grooves of muscles, veins bulging under strain, pores and scars visible; skin dents at the moment of impact and bruises surface.
- **Hair / fabric**: during a fight, strands / sleeves / capes are pulled straight by the airflow, matted with sweat, and swing with physical inertia; fabric really sways and tears with the movement, a cloak is lifted by the wind of a punch — never let hair sit rigid like plastic sheets, never let clothes ignore the movement.
- **Weapons / metal**: highlights, scratches, rolled edges, chips; blades spark on collision; a weapon biting in meets resistance, deforms and gives feedback — not just "pasted on and done".
- **Environment**: the ground cracks on impact, walls dent, rubble sprays, dust rises; wooden crates / stone pillars / glass really shatter; high-speed slides leave friction marks.
- Counter-examples: never show brand-new unworn weapons, never show impacts with no environmental feedback, never let VFX glow free of any material.

## Silhouette and proportion language
- Body shape is a declaration of power: wiry / explosive hero, power-type brute, agile low-body-fat type — use silhouette and mass difference to intensify the confrontation. Never give everyone the same body template; never let a frail character swing power that does not match their mass (unless the setting says so).
- Every character has a recognisable combat signature: a unique weapon form, a trademark opening stance, a personal habit of generating force (left-hand blade / low sliding step / shoulder-first breach).
- Costume serves movement: tight combat wear / bound sleeves expose the limb trajectory; flowing capes / belts amplify the sense of speed and force. Injuries accumulate persistently (wounds / blood / damage remain in later shots); never let everyone be "back to full health" in every shot.

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
### A. Iron rules of speed and opening (prevent sluggishness)
1. **Fight from second 0, no slow open**: from the first frame this style must be in effective motion or high-tension standoff; no long meaningless standing stare-downs / slow entrances / empty environment shots used to pad time (a ≤1 s wind-up is allowed). The very first frame should already carry "about to explode" tension or be in motion.
2. **Four-beat force transmission chain**: every strike completes "wind-up → drive → impact → feedback"; missing one beat voids the action. Wind-up states the preparation (shoulder drop / hip twist / pull back / lower the centre of gravity), drive states the transmission path (push off the ground → rotate hips → deliver the shoulder), impact states the contact point, feedback states deformation / displacement / shattering.
3. **High speed must be visible**: translate "fast" into on-screen evidence (ground exploding, rubble flying backwards / clothes and long hair pulled straight / afterimages and motion trails / background parallax blur / running up a wall to turn / mid-air direction change / shockwave sweeping debris away); never just shout "extremely fast / devastating".
4. **Ban slow words**: ban stalling phrases like "slowly / gently / sidesteps / catches it steadily / smooth and continuous / quickly retreats" that never land on a concrete displacement; actions must happen "swish" and produce a result.

### B. Attack-defence interaction (prevent turn-based fighting, prevent punching bags)
5. **Attack and defence in sync, no turn-based rhythm**: on the same frame one side punches, the other has already blocked and counterattacks; a dodge cuts directly into the next strike. Never "A attacks → B slowly reacts → B attacks".
6. **The opponent must counter**: the enemy must actively attack, block, pursue or counter — never stand still and take hits like a punching bag; at least one effective counter must force the hero to dodge / change line / leap / fall.
7. **Combo relay**: the recovery of the previous strike becomes the wind-up of the next; the opponent's block / hit / dodge decides the next attack line. Never stack move names without contact / deformation / displacement.
8. **Every hit has a consequence**: whoever is hit must lose balance, be displaced, fall, roll, stagger back, or crash into the environment; never write only "hits" without what follows.

### C. Weapon-platform and VFX discipline (prevent plastic fighting)
9. **Combat platform routing (by each character's actual condition)**: unarmed = fists, palms, elbows, knees, legs, grappling, close dodges, throws; bladed = slash, thrust, flick, parry, deflect, cutting wounds and cross-sections; polearms = reach advantage, levering, sweeps, reverse thrusts; blunt = smash, crush, ram, denting, big knockback; ranged = clear ballistics / beams / cover / firing intervals. If there is no weapon, stay unarmed — never conjure blades or guns out of nothing; a weapon's form stays consistent throughout, never morph / vanish / duplicate / swap hands without cause.
10. **Attack-defence matchup**: armed vs unarmed → the unarmed side relies on side slips, closing in, controlling the arm, gripping non-sharp parts, kicking the wrist, shoulder checks, throws and counters; a bare hand must never catch a blade unharmed (unless the setting allows it). Ability types → abilities are triggered by real actions, with a source, a direction and a dissipation; never two blobs of light trading beams, never ranged VFX with no source.
11. **VFX ownership discipline**: sword light / battle qi / lightning / crystals / barriers must attach to concrete actions and collisions, with trigger source + direction of travel + dissipation result; never let VFX fly around out of nowhere, never let VFX hide the core exchange.
12. **Spatial displacement and non-repetition**: every exchange changes at least one of position / direction / height / speed / posture / attack-defence relation / environment structure; never brawl in place, never repeat the same move / attack line / composition / camera move. Cut while the action is unfinished (blade just contacting / body mid-rotation / mid-flight from a hit); after the cut never re-set the stance / re-draw the sword / teleport without cause; the next segment must inherit the previous segment's end state (position / facing / speed / hit state / lingering dust).

### D. Iron rules of fight density (CRITICAL: the core of preventing turn-based, sluggish fights)
Following the writing of cinematic fight choreography, a fight in this style must make the screen **look like it is really fighting** — **the rhythm comes from "several combo beats inside one shot + a causal chain throughout", not from "one move each, one cut each"**. Execute every item below:
1. **Causal-chain directing structure (one through-line for the whole segment, not scattered action stacking)**: seize space proactively → the opponent counters the real line → contact produces force and displacement → displacement creates a new height / angle / opening → immediate pursuit → attack-defence reversal → counter-kill or finish → outcome and environmental aftershock. Each step is the result of the previous one and the origin of the next.
2. **Several consecutive beats inside one shot (prevent turn-based)**: within one [Shot N] complete ≥3 effective attack-defence beats in a row (e.g. slash → block → change line into an upward cut → dodge → counter-thrust → knocked apart → reposition), driving "three or more consecutive moves" into the same shot; **never "one move each then cut" turn-based fighting**. Cuts happen only at unfinished relay points (see §C12); after the cut the next shot immediately continues from the knocked-away posture, never re-setting the guard.
3. **Write the full force loop for every exchange**: an attack must have an opening → a clear line → a clear contact point → the opponent defending / dodging / countering according to their own condition → deformation under force (weapon bending / muscles trembling / clothes and hair swinging) → displacement (step back / side slide / airborne / hitting the ground) → environmental reaction (dust bursting / rubble spraying / cracks in the ground). **Never write only "hits / blocks / cuts out" with no loss of balance or displacement for the receiver** (that is exactly why a fight looks fake, slow and weightless).
4. **No waiting, no pausing**: after passing each other / being knocked apart, **pursue immediately** (never wait for the opponent to steady themselves, never insert a breathing empty shot); never a "hit-and-pause" beat; standoffs / wind-ups may last ≤1 s as pre-burst setup and must carry real tension (muscles tightening / weapon trembling slightly / debris pushed away by pressure), never a long static stare.
5. **Attack and defence in sync, both sides moving**: while one side attacks, the other must already be blocking / dodging / changing line to counter (see §B5); never stand still and take hits, never wait for the opponent to finish before reacting lazily.
6. **Three-beat finish (termination structure)**: the deciding blow closes with "force the guard → change line → impact" (first press the opponent into a predictable defence with a frontal move, then, exploiting that their weight and line are already committed, abruptly change line and axis, and finally hit from the true direction); after the impact clearly show the loser being knocked away / crashing into the environment / losing the weapon / collapsing to one knee — a **visible result**; never end with a soft exchange and both sides fine.
7. **Fast cutting that follows the fight**: fight segments cut fast and stay close (close-up on the contact point / medium shot for the whole move / whip pans following displacement), each shot carrying clear spatial relations; never use slow lateral moves or long empty shots that drag the rhythm; high-speed sections may use local slow motion to magnify "seeing the fatal blow", but return to full speed immediately after (see the shot-preset notes).

### E. Close-quarters exchange and impact-feedback density (prevent "one wide shot then cut", prevent "floaty and weightless")
> A fight has two kinds of spectacle: **wide / full shots show the big moves** (leaping / spinning / axe kicks / flying kicks); **close / medium-close shots show "killing intent and density"** (interlocking fists and feet / blocks bouncing off / face-to-face combos). A fight in this style must alternate both; never only wide-shot big swings (= fake fighting with no density). Execute every item below:
1. **Keep a face-to-face close-quarters exchange (prevent dilution by wide shots)**: every fight must contain at least one 2-3 second upper-body close / medium-close exchange that writes out a **continuous move sequence** (who, with what move, striking which body part, how the other dodges / blocks / parries / retreats). Wide-shot swinging alone does not count as a complete fight.
2. **Serialise the moves (prevent abstract frequency words)**: close-quarters exchange must never use unfilmable qualitative words like "high-speed flurry / dense punching / one punch after another / fists blurring into one"; it must be written as **a chain of concrete filmable moves + impact points + the opponent's reaction each time**, e.g. "right hook to the face → left uppercut to the chin → right straight to the bridge of the nose → left horizontal elbow across the brow → right spear hand to the throat; the opponent leans aside, ducks, parries, bridges, retreats in turn". Never use abstract parameters like "≥X attacks per second / ≤X seconds between hits".
3. **Shot size matches action amplitude (prevent "small action in a wide shot = invisible")**: large actions (leap / spin / axe kick / sweep / flying kick) use full / medium shots to show the whole body and the space; small actions (fists, palms and elbows at face range / inch punches / blocks) use close-ups to magnify density. Small action in a wide shot = diluted and unreadable; big action only in close-up = no tension. Close → wide must pass through a medium transition; wide → close may compress in two steps (a lunge carries its own sense of speed).
4. **Camera purposefulness (prevent dead shots)**: normal-speed exchange = medium-close profile with slight movement (camera pushes in slightly with the punch, jolts back slightly on the block); a big wind-up = low angle + slow push-in (let the audience feel "something big is coming" before the burst); fast attack-defence = oblique angle + camera moving in the same direction as the attack (speed comes from the camera, not from shouting "fast"); close-up = emotional punctuation for an opening / turning point, 0.2-0.5 s then cut away — never use a close-up as a normal zoomed long shot.
5. **Air and impact feedback on every strike (prevent "floaty")**: light ≠ weak — write air feedback for every strike (the wind of a fist scattering dust / a leg cutting the air / hair and sleeves blown up); write the burst on impact (a ripple of air for a light hit / a sonic surge for a heavy hit / a shockwave for the finisher); write the passive feedback of the receiver (air bouncing off the block / the air behind them pushed out / a slide leaving two dust trails / hair blown forward by the blast); after heavy hits write the aftershock flowing back (dust drawn back in / the air still trembling). Prove speed with **afterimages and air streaks** (afterimage / arc-shaped air streak / sonic cone), never just shout "extremely fast".
6. **Micro-feedback on impact (close-up realism)**: in close-quarters exchanges write 1-2 micro-feedbacks that make the pain visible — the tremor of a block (arm quivering / the fist stopping an inch from the face), a guard shattering open, sweat drops flung off, a line or mist of blood, pupils contracting sharply, blood at the corner of the mouth; never let both sides finish clean and undamaged.
7. **Escalate as the fight goes on (prevent uniform force all the way)**: pressure the whole fight round by round — speed (medium → fast → extreme), force (light → heavy → extreme), attack mode (conventional → vicious → lethal), impact zone (torso → ribs → chin → throat), reaction to being hit (slight jolt → doubling over → head snapping back → knocked flying), environmental damage (scuff → crack → rubble → shatter → collapse). The audience must see the escalation curve "can block → can't block → knocked away"; never uniform effort from start to finish.
8. **Three states of defence (prevent the fake "taking hits calmly")**: as the impact intensity rises, the defender goes through "intact (calm blocking) → broken guard (the block shattered, both arms flung aside, chest and abdomen exposed for 0.3-0.5 s) → transitional (no time to rebuild a full guard, only fragmented defence: side slip / lean back / one-arm parry / step back / curl up, posture messy)"; within 0.3-1 s after the guard is broken they must not instantly return to calm full blocking. The blocking method changes with the attack type (straight punch → palm deflection; curved hook → cross both arms to seal; close elbow → lift both arms and retreat; low sweep → raise the knee; spinning kick → duck under it), and the post-block posture must lead straight into the counter.
9. **Correct use of breathing frames / slow motion (prevent abusing freeze frames)**: slow motion or freeze frames are used in exactly two places — ① an extremely slow "breathing frame" 0.3-0.5 s before a finisher (sweat drops / hair / debris suspended, creating an extremely-still → extremely-violent contrast), **only if at least 1.5 s of high-speed footage precedes it**; ② a very short slow motion at the instant of impact to magnify "seeing the fatal blow". **A freeze or slow-motion beat without high-speed setup = a boring pause, forbidden**; return to fast rhythm immediately after. The final shot of a fight is the **physical end state** = a visible freeze of the climax result (the loser knocked away / crashed into the environment / weapon lost / collapsed to one knee), not a calm sheathing of the sword or hair drifting down.

### F. Realistic weapon and immortal-arts fighting (used by the sub-textures "impressionistic wuxia flow / immortal-arts fantasy flow")
> Execute this section only when the segment involves **weapons (saber / sword / spear / polearm / scythe)** or **immortal-arts fantasy (flying swords / sword qi / formations / spell barrages)**; skip it for unarmed modern combat. Make "filmable action + blade/energy trajectory + force feedback" the body of the shot; never paste on a "sword qi / VFX" layer and call it done. Execute every item below:
1. **Five-layer anchoring for immortal arts / divine powers (prevent fake VFX and abstraction)**: any weapon technique or immortal-arts move must state five layers — **who strikes, where the energy/weapon starts, along what trajectory, where it hits, what deformation / displacement / shattering it causes**; never write only an abstract technique name with no executable action detail (e.g. "unleashes the Ten-Thousand Swords Return" ✗). Flying swords / sword qi must state the positional relation between person and sword (hovering / at hand / long range); sword qi / formations must have structural levels (gather / stack / rotate / joint kill); never two blobs of light trading beams.
2. **Real feedback for weapons**: saber / sword parry → sparks, a metallic ring, blade edges biting and pushing; a cut landing → resistance, a cutting path, a fractured surface; polearms → reach advantage, levering, sweeps, reverse thrusts; a weapon's form stays consistent throughout — never morph / vanish / duplicate / swap hands without cause; when a weapon is knocked aside, show it being lost or the wielder's hand going numb. A blade cutting air makes a sound (sword hum / whistle / blade wind); a blade-less weapon must never produce a sword hum.
3. **The stronger the move, the more restrained it must be**: a big move does not have to fill the screen with explosions — **tiny action + pinpoint killing intent + rock-steady camera** conveys a higher realm (e.g. formless sword intent, formless instantaneous draw). For "redirect / borrow force" moves, write an arc that guides the incoming force (a palm touch → deflect along the force → the opponent's centre of gravity is carried away); never write a hard block.
4. **High altitude / big moves must establish spatial scale**: flying swords / leaps / dives / high-altitude finishers need at least one medium → full shot stating the height and the path (how high the person is, where the move starts, where they dive, where they land); never keep everything in close-up where the depth is unreadable. Camera movement must serve the genre (impressionistic wuxia = whip pans + slow motion; immortal arts = slow motion + light trails).
5. **Impressionistic wuxia rhythm (impressionistic wuxia flow)**: wind-up extremely still (within 0.5 s, unmoving as a mountain / eyes locked / sword tip resting on the ground) → the strike extremely dynamic (one move and the position is exchanged / a flash of sword light arrives) → freeze on the recovery; qinggong / body technique uses **visible leverage** (touching water / stepping on a branch / pushing off a wall / turning in mid-air); airborne position changes have continuous trajectories, never teleport or flash without cause; afterimages may convey speed, but solid clones are forbidden by default (unless the setting has an illusion system).
6. **Move names may prompt but must not stack**: you may write a move name as a guide, e.g. "the sword walks the three-fold startling swan, tapping the throat in succession", but **every move name must be followed by filmable action detail** (trajectory / impact point / opponent's reaction / force), never a bare list of move names (see §B7 combo relay).

### G. Beats inside a shot · secondary motion · visible-fact density (mandatory in this style)
- **Beats** (use blank lines to split several beats inside one shot; each beat = trigger → action → result): opening (≤1 s) → receiving / changing line → impact and deformation → displacement / knock-apart → recovery or environmental reaction. ≥3 beats per shot; never write it as one flat run-on paragraph.
- **Secondary-motion checklist** (carry at least one per action, stating direction / amplitude / material): pack and ornaments swinging, hem / cape / fur collar whipping, hair and hairband trembling, weapon afterimages and broken blade fragments, ground dust / leaves / water splashing up, sparks and particles carried off by the shockwave.
- **Visible-fact density**: in fight segments, **≥3 filmable facts per second** (limb centre of mass / contact point / deformation / displacement / weapon state / facial muscles / environmental reaction / camera follow / synchronised sound).
- **Noun modifiers (style permeation)**: high-saturation warm light, stylised trailing energy arcs, particle sparks, speed lines / impact rings, hard-edged shadows — every environment / VFX / prop noun must carry this kind of modifier.
- **Action-loop scaling**: this item runs at **full amplitude** (the same density iron rules as §D/§E).

## Action performance and expression
- Complete beats of preparation → burst → recovery; 1-2 frames of body deformation and facial distortion at the moment of impact are the key to "realism"; being hit produces real deformation and displacement (abdomen denting / head snapping back / sliding back from the blow).
- Combat emotion levels: standoff tension (pupils contracting / veins bulging / teeth clenched), burst release (roaring), gritting through a setback, resolve and exhaustion at the decider. Never one "serious face" the whole way; never fight with a blank face.
- Expression in sync with force: the face is dragged by the force on a heavy blow, neck veins bulge when roaring, pain and defiance coexist when hit, the eyes stay locked on the opponent. Never let emotion detach from action (roaring but throwing a weightless punch).

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Cut rhythm: fast cutting in action passages; slow-shot setup for wind-ups / standoffs (no more than 1 s); cut to close-up on impact; fast rhythm for chases and consecutive attacks; a 1 s environment shot between exchanges may serve as breath (falling sparks / cracked ground), but never several empty shots that slow everything down.
- Shot size: close-up (contact point / expression / hands / blade), medium (whole move), full (battlefield / standoff space); cut to close-up at the moment of impact to intensify it.
- Transition: mostly hard cuts; cross-dissolve for time jumps; no sluggish fades that drag the rhythm.
- Camera-move levels: push/pull = push toward the fist or blade tip, pull back to reveal the battlefield; pan/truck = sweep the battlefield / bamboo forest; tracking = follow the footwork / the chase; orbit = circle the two opponents / high-speed orbit; crane = immortal mountain / arena height; dive = strike from above; camera roll / fisheye impact = the moment of eruption.
- Layered rhythm (by genre): realistic physics is fast, precise and vicious; wuxia uses the contrast of extreme stillness and extreme motion (wind-up very still, strike very dynamic, freeze on recovery); xianxia goes from deep stillness in meditation to violent phenomena erupting. This style allows local slow motion to magnify key strikes, but slow motion must serve "seeing the fatal blow", and full speed resumes immediately after.

## Sound design
- Realistic physics: fist wind / bone-and-flesh impacts / metal clash / shattering / sleeves cutting air / footsteps / the flight and landing after a heavy hit. No metal means no sword hum.
- Wuxia: sword hum / sleeves / toes touching water / bamboo leaves; silence and breath are the soul of the impressionistic mode, but only in the instant of a standoff wind-up — sound returns immediately once the move is out.
- Xianxia: the hum of gathering qi / formation resonance / heavenly thunder / sonic booms / energy dissipating.
- Sound rhythm: the whoosh of the attack platform → the sound of real materials contacting → the sound of the body under force → the sound of environmental destruction → the sound of re-acceleration, following the action layer by layer; every exchange states which sound appears in sync with which action.

## Exclusive taboos
- Never stack move names without contact / deformation / displacement; never let the enemy stand still and take hits like a punching bag; never add weapons or superpowers without cause.
- Never let the hero reset without cause after a cut (re-set the stance / re-draw the sword / re-wind); never let the next shot fail to inherit the previous shot's end state.
- Never use empty shouty slow words like "extremely fast / devastating / slowly / catches it steadily / smooth and continuous / sidesteps / quickly retreats" (they must land on visible displacement and results).
- Never let two [Shot N] be almost identical; never repeat the same action description across shots; never let VFX appear detached from the action.
- Never allow a plastic feel (skin / hair / fabric / weapons / environment with no real feedback); never write a fight as a plot summary ("the two started fighting").
- Never apply this style to a purely lyrical story with no confrontation (that should use Warm Slice-of-Life and similar styles).

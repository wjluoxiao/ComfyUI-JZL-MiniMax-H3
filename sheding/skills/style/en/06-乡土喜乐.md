## Style positioning
Recommended story types: rural comedy, street-life comedy, family farce, lighthearted sweet comedy, absurd coincidence, ensemble comedy, dialect comedy.
One-line focus: **the laugh comes from situation and body language, not from lines** — put the comedic beats of "misunderstanding, coincidence, a boast exposed, chase farce" at the centre of the frame. This style gives "setup of the joke → detonation → onlooker reaction" the most screen time, with a buoyant rhythm, exaggerated expressions and comedic movement.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, redistribute the emphasis
- Never change the misunderstandings, coincidences, characters or ending of the user's story; this style changes the allocation: amplify the comedic performance and reactions and make the rhythm brisker.
- If the user writes "he made a fool of himself" → expand it into a complete comedy passage: setup (deadly serious) → detonation (the accident happens) → exaggerated reaction (eyes bulging / falling over / onlookers roaring with laughter).
- Amplify "onlooker reactions": the villagers' / passers-by's laughter, whispers, eyes wide as saucers, mouths open wide enough to stuff an egg.
- Jokes rest on situation and body: a tumble, a chain of misunderstandings, a boast exposed, a chase; dialogue is a support, not the main engine.
- Keep the warm core of the user's story (the village warmth / human feeling inside the comedy), but never let sentimentality interrupt the rhythm.
- Do not invent plot, change the ending, or force in gags — what is expanded is the "exaggeration and rhythm of the comedic performance", not the story itself.
- When the user's story has no joke, never manufacture comedy that changes the direction of the story; you may amplify the drama already present while staying faithful.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: high-saturation rustic / street-life realism; a deep blue sky, golden rice fields, fiery red chillies / vivid shop signs; the "sweating buckets" feel of harsh midday sun; warm yellow bulbs / firelight at night.
- No polished urban-drama filters, no over-refinement that destroys the down-to-earth feel.
- Camera: locked-off shots simulating the "villager watching the fun at the village entrance" viewpoint; crash zooms to magnify embarrassment; wide low angles for exaggerated falls and chases; generous close-ups on expressions.

## Material texture (the real, down-to-earth touch)
- The physical touch of the countryside: mottled mud-brick houses, the way a scarecrow in the rice field is lashed together, the bustle of a market stall, flagstones under the big banyan tree at the village entrance, coarse cloth on the clothesline.
- The everyday warmth of props: a scuffed paint job on a three-wheeler with a straw mat in the bed, the sheen of oil in an iron wok, chips in an enamel mug, a loudspeaker, a straw hat, an undershirt, the patina on old furniture.
- Street-life comedy may use brighter everyday props (steam from food stalls, neon signs, grocery shelves) while keeping the real, lived-in feel.
- Counter-examples: never over-refine and kill the rustic / street flavour; never let props look like an advertisement set.

## Silhouette and proportion language
- Exaggerated rustic designs: the boastful village chief (Mao suit / straw hat), the shrewd village beauty, the honest strongman, the busybody auntie, the henpecked husband — recognisability through exaggerated traits.
- Street-life comedy: down-to-earth designs for ordinary people (undershirt / apron / uniform), vivid and memorable.
- Expression and body language are part of the design: eyes bulging like bells, a mouth open wide enough to stuff an egg, the contrast of round bodies, a comically bedraggled look.
- Never a polished idol-style design for the characters (it weakens the comedy and the realism).

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
A comedy beat = the drop of "deadly serious setup → accidental detonation → half-beat pause → onlookers roaring"; the laugh comes from **situation, misunderstanding and body language**, not from wisecracking lines. Buoyant rhythm, exaggerated expressions (+30 %), comedic movement. The sections below are organised by theme — execute every item:

### A. The contrast of setup and detonation (prevent "a joke with no setup")
1. **Deadly serious setup (CRITICAL)**: the more serious, the more sworn, the more confident the character is before the joke, the funnier the detonation — first make the audience believe "he's going to pull it off", then let him crash on the spot. The contrast between setup and detonation is the core rhythm of this style; never have someone fall over for no reason.
2. **30 % exaggeration creates comedy**: bulging eyes, open mouths, tumbles and chases are exaggerated on top of reality; expressions and actions must be about 30 % larger than life to land (less is flat, more is cringe).
   - Good: "His foot slips and he goes sprawling head over heels into the haystack; chaff bursts up and only his two flailing legs stick out."
   - Bad: "He falls over" (flat, no comedy, ✗).
3. **Exaggeration needs elasticity and clumsiness**: a fall is not "flopping down with a smack" but the process of "trip → stagger → struggle → sprawl"; chases have sudden stops, running into doors, getting tripped, skidding turns — the "loss of control" inside the movement is the joke; never let the embarrassment be executed cleanly and neatly.

### B. Situational misunderstanding and chains (prevent "wisecracking")
4. **Chains of situational misunderstanding (prevent relying on witty lines)**: the laugh comes from misunderstanding and chain reactions — a boast busted on the spot, one sentence passed on wrong, one lie needing ten lies to cover, coincidences piling up, digging the hole deeper; push with the structure "misunderstanding — patch-up — bigger misunderstanding"; never carry the comedy on internet memes or wisecracks.
5. **Three beats of a busted boast**: the boast (getting bigger and bigger) → the omen of exposure (the audience / accomplices start noticing, mouths twitching) → the bust on the spot (the witness and the evidence happen to appear); all three beats are needed; at the instant of exposure give the hero one frame of "freezing / turning to stone".
6. **Comic rhythm of coincidence**: coincidences must be "just in time but not forced" — interlocking so the audience realises "something is about to go wrong" before the character does (anticipatory pleasure), and then the coincidence lands.

### C. Three reaction beats and amplified onlookers (prevent "a joke with no response")
7. **Three reaction beats (CRITICAL, the valve of the joke)**: after the accident, first **half a beat of silence** (everyone frozen / eyes bulging) → then the eruption (roaring laughter / hands over mouths / slapping thighs / rocking back and forth) — this half-beat pause is the valve, giving the audience "reaction time"; never cut away the moment the accident happens.
8. **The onlookers' viewpoint**: locked-off shots let the audience feel like villagers watching the fun at the village entrance; the synchronised reactions of the crowd (first silence → roaring / whispering / wiping tears / slapping thighs) amplify the joke, so give full coverage to group shots; use crash zooms to instantly magnify the character's embarrassment / smugness / exposure / fright.

### D. Human warmth (prevent "cold after the laugh")
9. **Keep the warm core, touch and move on**: intersperse moments of true feeling inside the farce (family and neighbours helping each other, a harsh mouth with a soft heart, making up after the row), but **never sentimental, never interrupt the rhythm** — warmth is a passing touch, then straight back to the fun; the undertone of comedy is warm human feeling, not meanness.
10. **Relationships are the source of the jokes**: the boastful village chief running into the shrewd village beauty, the henpecked husband showing off in public, the honest strongman being handled by the aunties — use fixed relationships to create predictable jokes, and let the same pair collide again and again (the more familiar, the funnier).

### E. Beats inside a shot · secondary motion · visible-fact density (mandatory in this style)
- **Beats** (use blank lines to split several beats inside one shot; each beat = trigger → action → result): setup (normal rhythm / everyday action) → detonation (accident / misunderstanding / an object getting out of control) → three reaction beats (the hero → the bystanders → the environment "joining the mess") → deflation / wrap-up.
- **Secondary-motion checklist**: a straw hat or bamboo hat tumbling, trouser cuffs and a faded cloth shirt flapping, a carrying pole and baskets swaying, chickens scattering and dogs barking / livestock flicking their tails, dust / water splashing from a bucket / vegetable leaves flying, apron ties and handkerchiefs.
- **Visible-fact density**: ≥2 filmable facts per second (amplitude of exaggerated limbs, objects slipping and colliding, the chain of expressions in the crowd, chain reactions in the environment); "awkward / lively" must be shown through visible action and reaction.
- **Noun modifiers (style permeation)**: high-saturation earth tones / warm yellow, hard light with dust particles, exaggerated perspective — every environment / prop noun must carry this kind of modifier.
- **Action-loop scaling**: **exaggerated amplitude (about 30 % over the top)** — collisions / slips / shoves must be written out with full force and chain effects, while still obeying this style's "the joke needs a setup, the reaction needs three beats".

## Action performance and expression (layered performance)
- Emotions are written on the face and exaggerated: smugness while boasting, embarrassment when exposed, bulging eyes when caught out, sprawled limbs when falling; never inward.
- Comic rhythm: movement is slightly faster than real life, pauses are used for reaction (freeze first, then laugh); falls and chases have elasticity and clumsiness.
- Ensemble reactions: the synchronised expressions of onlookers (first silence → roaring laughter / rocking back and forth / wiping tears) amplify the joke.
- Emotional grading keeps human warmth: moments of true feeling are interspersed in the farce (family and neighbours helping each other), but only in passing, never sentimental.

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Cut rhythm: fast-paced comedy, average shot length 2-3 s; hold a funny expression / reaction for 1 s then cut; chases use an accelerating feel with a comic beat; long takes follow the chaos in farce scenes.
- Shot size: medium (the whole farce), close-up (exaggerated expressions / bulging eyes / open mouths / the instant of exposure), wide (chases and falls).
- Transition: crisp hard cuts between jokes; wipes may be used for scene changes (village entrance / market / field / doorway).
- Camera-move levels: push/pull = crash zoom to magnify embarrassment; pan/truck = sweep the village entrance / market / living room; tracking = follow the bumpy chase; handheld = the shake of a chase; one continuous take = a long take following the chase / farce.
- Lighting: harsh midday sun / warm yellow bulbs at night / firelight; plain and realistic — never carried by VFX.

## Sound design
- Ambience: roosters and dogs, the noise of the market, frogs and cicadas, a spatula in the wok, rural / street white noise, a loudspeaker broadcast.
- Comic effects: the thud of a fall, running footsteps, exaggerated panting, the crowd's roar of laughter, a sharp intake of breath, a dropped cup, a slapped table, a toppled stool.
- Lively with a real sense of life; the sudden silence at the joke and the laughter that follows is the key to this style's sound rhythm.
- Score preference (if specified): cheerful folk music / suona / brisk rhythm / comic effects; when music is banned, rely on ambience to carry the comedy.

## Exclusive taboos
- Never lose the rustic / street feel (shooting it as a polished urban drama); never under-exaggerate expressions and body language.
- Never rest the jokes entirely on wisecracking dialogue; never change the user's story ending or warm human core for the sake of a laugh.
- Never over-refine and kill the down-to-earth feel; never turn comedy into misery or over-sentimentality (warmth is a passing touch).
- Never invent plot, force in gags, or cram in internet memes — stay faithful to the misunderstandings and jokes the user's story is built on.
- Never apply the action genre's "steady, precise, vicious, fast" iron rules or the gloomy rhythm of horror / suspense.

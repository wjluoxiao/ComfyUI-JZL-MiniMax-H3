## Style positioning
Recommended story types: CEO sweet romance, counterattack and face-slapping, transmigration and rebirth, hidden-identity boss, playing the fool to eat the tiger, urban power fantasy, wealthy-family marriage drama.
One-line focus: **contrast and face-slapping are the engine of this style** — put the satisfying beats of "looked down on → identity revealed → everyone shocked → relationship reversed" at the centre of the frame. Given the same story, this style gives the "reveal moment, onlooker reactions, status flip, sweet interaction" the most screen time and the densest shots.

This style contains two sub-textures that snap to the subject automatically:
- **Sweet-romance flow** (CEO / sweet romance / marriage): cold public authority vs gentle private tenderness — the contrast, plus the gazes and touches of romantic moments.
- **Power-fantasy flow** (counterattack / face-slapping / rebirth / hidden identity): the strong contrast between being suppressed and turning the tables, the shock of the reveal moment and the onlookers' astonishment.

## Core principle: faithful to the plot, redistribute the emphasis
- Never change the identity, turning points, relationship direction or ending of the user's story; this style changes the allocation: give "contrast and reaction shots" the largest share.
- If the user writes "he is actually a hidden boss" → expand it into a full reveal passage: doubt → reveal (magnified in slow motion) → the crowd's shocked reaction → the opponent losing composure → the hero unmoved.
- Amplify "onlooker reactions": staring eyes / sharp intakes of breath / whispered gossip / half a step back / a dropped glass — these amplify the satisfaction and the sweetness, so give them full coverage.
- Keep the setup before the reversal (the grievance of being looked down on / misunderstood) and give it full weight so the reversal has a drop to fall from; after the reversal give the afterglow of "commanding the room / sweetness".
- Keep the meaning of the dialogue; the face-slap and heart-flutter moments are shown through image and reaction, not through lines in which the hero announces himself.
- Do not invent plot, change identity settings, or force in twists — what is expanded is the "presentation density of contrast and reaction", not the story itself.

## Visual style (including palette & lighting, cinematography)
- Rendering: fashion / urban photorealism (sweet romance may lean into high-fashion editorial); depending on the genre you may use live-action realism or fantasy VFX (hidden-identity boss / fantasy may use golden light).
- Palette: **contrast grading** — suppression / setback passages are low-saturation cool grey with harsh top light and dark shadow; reveal / highlight passages are warm golden light with a heroic backlit silhouette / saturated splendour. The instant of identity revelation is intensified with contrast in material and light.
- Lighting: the first half is oppressive (cool grey / shadow), the second half is glorious (warm gold / side backlight); romantic scenes get warm accents (candle gold / sunset orange-pink / deep rose red).
- Camera: high angle = smallness while suppressed; low angle + backlight = stature and aura after the counterattack; the reveal moment uses an extremely fast push into a facial close-up + dilating pupils; first meeting / reunion / romantic moments use slow-motion push-ins to magnify the meeting of eyes.
- Composition: formal / power settings are symmetrical (high-angle authority), private / sweet spaces are asymmetrical (eye level, relaxed).

## Material texture (the evidence of luxury and contrast)
- Macro shots of luxury goods build the high-end feel (sweet-romance flow): the weave of a tailored suit, the gears and sapphire crystal of a wristwatch, a high heel on marble, the facets of a diamond ring, the city skyline beyond a floor-to-ceiling window, leather interiors.
- Contrasting materials as narrative (power-fantasy flow): the first half is shabby and cheap (washed-out old clothes / cold leftovers / a dim corner / cheap cloth shoes); after the reveal it is opulent and sharp (fine clothes / golden radiance / textured accessories) — the instant of revelation is intensified by contrast in material.
- Environmental detail: cold white glass curtain walls and dark grey metal in the office, warm yellow wood panelling and soft fabric in the mansion, crystal chandeliers at the banquet, bidding paddles at the auction house.
- Counter-examples: never lose the high-end feel (luxury goods / suit texture is the baseline), never cheap filters or over-beautified plastic looks, and never let it stay dim and cheap after the counterattack.

## Silhouette and proportion language
- Sweet-romance costume opposition: the CEO in a dark grey suit / a silver-grey sports car / sharp lines / a wristwatch; the heroine in soft colours, refined but natural. Composition is the relationship — office symmetry (power) vs private-space asymmetry (relaxation).
- Power-fantasy before/after costume contrast: while suppressed, shabby clothes and a shrinking posture (rounded shoulders / lowered head / tucked into a corner); after the comeback, crisp fine clothes and full aura (upright / backlit / sharp eyes).
- The hero carries visual foreshadowing of a "hidden identity" (low-key but refined details, a bearing unlike others); the opponent is flashy and arrogant; the bystanders are the amplifiers of the payoff.
- Never a one-note cold CEO (his private tenderness must be filmable); never let him stay dim and shabby after the comeback.

## Core directing grammar (executed item by item in this style)
The engine of this style = the drop-and-rise beat of "suppressed / looked down on → identity or feeling revealed → the crowd's shock or heart-flutter → commanding the room / sweet afterglow". The bigger the drop, the greater the payoff; **reaction shots and contrasting visuals are the amplifiers**. The sections below are organised by theme — execute every item:

### A. Building the contrast (prevent "a counterattack with no drop")
1. **Contrast is the narrative (CRITICAL)**: the same character's before-and-after visual flip (high vs low angle / light and dark / symmetry of composition / material / the attitude of those around them) is itself the face-slap and the heart-flutter. The first half must fully establish and concretely film "being suppressed / looked down on / misunderstood" — not just being verbally despised, but sitting in the furthest corner, being ignored when speaking, being sneered at for shabby clothes, being publicly humiliated. Without a full buildup of grievance there is no satisfying drop.
2. **Visual foreshadowing of the hidden identity**: while suppressed, the hero still carries one "low-key but refined" detail (stitching on a cuff / a good watch under old clothes / an unruffled gaze / the way he holds a teacup) so that "not simple" is traceable and the reveal does not feel abrupt; the opponent and passers-by must be flashy and arrogant (chin up, sideways glance, loud mockery) to build the charge for the face-slap.

### B. Detonating the reveal (prevent "glossing over it")
3. **Magnify the reveal moment (CRITICAL, the heaviest beat of the power-fantasy flow)**: at the instant the identity or feeling is revealed, push in extremely fast to a facial close-up + dilating pupils, optionally with backlight / golden light / the whole room falling suddenly silent; **never gloss over the reveal with one line of dialogue** — the reveal needs the trio of "slow motion + close-up + abrupt environmental change" to carry the impact. If the reveal comes with an action (showing the token / demonstrating power / casting off the disguise), give the action its complete process rather than announcing it in dialogue.
4. **Reveal through image and reaction, not self-declaration**: at the face-slap / heart-flutter moment, prove it primarily through the crowd's reaction + the hero's composed body language; never use self-revealing lines like "actually I am the president of XX company"; the hero's reaction is "calm composure" (lifting the eyes / a slight lift at the corner of the mouth / unhurried / stepping slowly forward), not screaming.

### C. Amplifying onlookers and reactions (prevent "missing reaction shots")
5. **Give the onlooker reactions fully (CRITICAL, the amplifier of the payoff)**: every face-slap / reveal is paired with a group of reaction shots — staring eyes / open mouths / half a step backwards / a sharp intake of breath / whispered gossip / a slipping glass / someone rising and knocking over a chair; **there must be no fewer reaction shots than hero-action shots**, so that the shock is shouted for you by the crowd.
6. **Close-ups of the opponent losing composure**: the face-slapped opponent must go from arrogance to disbelief to losing composure (trembling hands / face draining white / speechless / stumbling back); the more concrete the loss of composure, the greater the payoff; never let the opponent shrug off a public humiliation.

### D. The afterglow of sweetness and control (prevent "disperse as soon as it's revealed")
7. **Slow-motion romantic moments (sweet-romance flow)**: first meeting / reunion / confession / heart-flutter use slow motion + push-in to magnify the meeting of eyes, a hand suspended in mid-air, a coat placed on shoulders, fingertips drawing closer, held breath; the romantic moment must "slow down so the audience sees the instant the heart flutters".
8. **Power vs tenderness composition contrast**: public settings (office / banquet / boardroom) use symmetrical composition + low angles for authority, a low voice, steady steps, a sweeping gaze; private settings (home / car / late at night) use eye level / warm light for tenderness, a softened voice, light movements, a moment of soft eyes — the contrast between the two states of the same character is the tension; never a uniformly cold CEO.
9. **The afterglow of commanding the room (ending)**: after the comeback give the hero shots of effortless control (walking slowly past a crowd that parts automatically / calmly adjusting a cuff / the opponent slinking away); after the sweetness give a freeze-frame of interaction (eye contact / holding hands / side by side / an embrace) to close the passage; end on the highlight moment of "being looked up to / being treated tenderly" rather than cutting away abruptly.
10. **"Doting / heart-flutter" must land on concrete actions**: taking off a coat and putting it over her shoulders, fingertips pausing for a second, tilting the umbrella toward her, brushing the stray hair from her forehead, bending down to tie a shoelace, remembering a small thing she said in passing and doing it — never a vague "he dotes on her" / "she was sweetened".

### E. Beats inside a shot · secondary motion · visible-fact density (mandatory in this style)
- **Beats** (use blank lines to split several beats inside one shot; each beat = trigger → action → result): contrast setup (old state / old object / old identity) → detonating the reveal (evidence / upgrade / reversal of position) → onlooker reactions (multiple viewpoints / chain of expressions) → afterglow of control (posture / tone / light closing the passage).
- **Secondary-motion checklist**: the hem and cuffs of a suit or trench coat, long hair or a braid swinging, skirt hems and high heels, the glint of jewellery / watches / tie clips, champagne bubbles, documents or business cards drifting down, the glow of a phone screen and a fingerprint on it.
- **Visible-fact density**: ≥2 filmable facts per second (height difference of postures, the intersection of sightlines, pointing gestures, an object entering the scene, the chain of onlooker expressions, a change of light); never substitute "a powerful aura / closing in step by step" for visible facts.
- **Noun modifiers (style permeation)**: high-end cool-grey tone, macro highlight edges, marble / metal reflections, backlit rim light on hair — every environment / prop noun must carry this kind of modifier.
- **Action-loop scaling**: **medium amplitude** — emphasise posture, aura and the weight of moving objects (rising and leaning forward / pushing a door / pulling a car door open / setting down a glass / opening a document) with full force and reaction.

## Action performance and expression (layered performance)
- Power-fantasy flow: while suppressed, endure in silence (clenched jaw / lowered eyes / tightened fist); at the reveal, calm composure (lifting the eyes / a slight lift at the corner of the mouth / unhurried); onlookers react in an exaggerated way.
- Sweet-romance flow: cold authority in public (low voice / steady steps / sweeping gaze), gentle tenderness in private (softened voice / light movements / a moment of soft eyes); feeling hides in the meeting of eyes, the lifted corner of the mouth, the protective body language.
- "Heart-flutter / doting" must land on concrete actions (taking off a coat and putting it on her shoulders, fingertips pausing for a second, tilting the umbrella, brushing the stray hair from her forehead); never a vague "he dotes on her".
- Never gloss over a reversal or heart-flutter moment; magnify it with image and reaction.

## Shot presets execution notes (works with the camera parameter settings)
- Cut rhythm: the early suppression is slow (silence + close-ups), the mid reversal erupts (fast cutting / extremely fast push-ins), the later control / sweetness is composed (slow motion); use hard cuts for contrast switches.
- Shot size: medium-close for the suppression passages (grievance), close-ups for the reveal (pupils / corner of the mouth / material detail), low-angle medium for the comeback (stature); always cut to close-ups for the crowd's shock.
- Transition: hard cuts between the face-slap and the comeback to create contrast; cross-dissolve for memories / identity reveals / passing time; fade for chapter breaks.
- Camera-move levels: push/pull = extremely fast push into the face at the instant of reversal; low angle = stature after the comeback; high angle = smallness while suppressed; orbit = circling the hero after the comeback / the heart-flutter eye contact; tracking = the composed exit / the chase for the wife.
- Lighting: a burst of golden light (the king returns), backlit silhouette, romantic warm accents; cool grey in the first half and warm gold in the second is the signature of this style.

## Sound design
- Ambience: the clamour of a banquet, office keyboards and high heels, the noise of the street, a luxury car engine / an elevator, bidding at an auction.
- Payoff effects: sharp intakes of breath, applause, a glass shattering, footsteps, whispers, the whole room falling suddenly silent (before the reveal).
- Sweet-romance effects: a heartbeat, breathing, glasses clinking, fabric rustling, high heels clicking, a phone message tone.
- The sound of the crowd's shock is the amplifier of the payoff; the high-end feel and romance rely on quiet and restraint — never a noisy soundtrack.

## Exclusive taboos
- Never a comeback without setup (the first half must fully establish being suppressed / misunderstood); never omit reaction shots.
- Never keep the grading dim and cheap after the comeback or the sweet reveal; never gloss over the face-slap or heart-flutter moment.
- Never a one-note cold CEO (that misses the private tenderness contrast); never lose the high-end feel (luxury detail / texture is the baseline).
- No melodramatic cringe acting, no cheap filters, no over-beautified plastic looks.
- Never invent plot, change identities, or force in twists — stay faithful to the user's story's contrast and relationship direction.
- Never apply the action genre's "steady, precise, vicious, fast" iron rules (this style runs on reaction and contrast, not on fighting).
